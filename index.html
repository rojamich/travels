{%- comment -%} ── On this day widget ───────────────────────────────────────────────── Two passes: (1) enumerate every past year that has posts, (2) for EACH year find that year's own closest-to-today post. Result: at most one card per year, each the nearest date to today from that year, newest year first. We keep the largest distance among the years shown so the subtitle can say whether these are exact matches or only near ones — every year on the nose → "Posts from 12 September in previous years", otherwise the looser "at this time" wording. A year only earns a card if its nearest post falls within otd_window days either side of today. Without that, a year we didn't travel in September still had to offer something, and "on this day" ended up showing a post from two months away. A year with nothing near today now simply doesn't appear, and if no year does, the widget hides itself rather than sitting there empty. Handles the year wrap (e.g. Jan 2 vs Dec 31 is 2 days, not 363) by taking the min of the raw distance and (365 - distance). Distance is measured on a fixed 365-day calendar built from the month and the day, NOT on strftime's %j. %j counts actual days elapsed, so a leap year shifts every date after 29 Feb along by one: 12 Sep 2024 is the 256th day but 12 Sep 2026 is the 255th, and comparing those two numbers says the same calendar date is a day apart. It showed: on 12 Sep 2026 the 2024 row offered a post dated 11 Sep over the one dated 12 Sep. Measuring month/day positions instead makes 12 Sep equal 12 Sep in every year. (29 Feb lands on the same slot as 1 Mar, which is as close as a 365-day calendar can get.) This runs on site.time — the BUILD time — so see the client-side pass further down, which redoes it against the reader's actual clock. {%- endcomment -%} {%- comment -%} Days elapsed before the 1st of each month in a common (non-leap) year, so month/day converts to a position with two additions. Used by the Liquid below and mirrored by the client-side pass. {%- endcomment -%} {%- comment -%} How near a post has to be, in days either side of today, to count as "on this day". {%- comment -%} ── Why there is a second, client-side pass ────────────────────────── Everything above runs on `site.time`, which is the moment Netlify built the site — NOT the moment somebody reads it. The build only happens when we push, so between deploys "today" is frozen. On 12 Sep 2026 the widget was still showing the Sep 5 matches, because Sep 5 was the last deploy. So: ship every past-year post's date to the browser and redo the same two-pass pick against the reader's own clock. The server-rendered cards above stay as the no-JavaScript fallback and the first paint; this only swaps them out when the real date picks something different. The payload is one entry per (year, date) — if two posts share a date only the first can ever win, exactly as in the Liquid loop, so the rest are dead weight. That is ~170 entries rather than one per post. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign otd_seen = "" | split: "" -%}
