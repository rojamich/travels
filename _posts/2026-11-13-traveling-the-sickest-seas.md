Holy seasickness batman! The evening of the 11th we began our journey back to Ushuaia to ensure we made it back before unsafe storms rolled in. Mike and I donned our sea sickness patches and bid our farewell to friends. Promising to remember them and wished them luck over the coming hard days 🤒😷🤢

Raquel, one of the bartenders slipped me a sleeve of saltines and we headed to our room, waters full, possessions strapped down, and phones stocked with audiobooks. The next 72 hours were some of the most miserable of our lives. The only time Mike and I have ever felt this sick together before (in our 15 years together!) was when we both had COVID and couldn't leave bed for days — completely exhausted.

Ingrid graciously supplied us with an extra pillow because I was still sick and having trouble breathing so I could sit up while sleeping. Mike had given me his pillow but now he would need one as we crossed over the passage. The first morning, Mike bravely fought to go to breakfast to get something in, thinking he felt decent 💪🏻He made it through and even managed to bring me some toast! 

After breakfast he said he was going to play cards and would bring me back tea. After finishing a card game, he ran down the stairs, seasick. He realized he forgot my tea and ran back upstairs (oh boy, just leave it honey!). He staggered back to our room but not without... tummy-related consequences.

For the next few days, we barely moved or spoke. We tried to sleep as much as possible and even put off drinking water to avoid having to hobble to the restroom. A few trips to the restroom resulted in us being catapulted across the room thanks to the 6 meter swells. At multiple times, I levitated off the bed and felt like I was in an exorcist movie. 

Mike showered and the water didn't stay in the shower stall. For the next 12 hours, we heard it slosh back and forth across the bathroom floor. There were announcements all day about classes offered, meal times, and views on deck. They only served as time markers for us and we went back to clutching our pillows. We routinely checked the tv that mapped our progress across the drakes passage and asked each other if 1) it looked like we had moved and 2) would this ever end?

The captain announced that we'd have to go an outer route around Isla de los Estados because the winds were so bad. On the way out of Ushuaia, we only suffered in the middle of the passage. This time, we didn't find relief until we were blocked, well within the Beagle Channel. After 48 hours of eating nothing but crackers, we finally ran out of food. We called the front desk (as recommended at the beginning of the cruise) and pled for carbs. They brough us a tray of breads, some fruit and some tea. Mike bravely fought towards the door and opened it, the employee looked horrified at our condition and quickly departed. 

We ate gratefully while lying down and trying to keep down bites of pear and buttered bread.

The evening of the 14th gave us shelter from the wailing winds that had cursed us for days and we meekly peered out of our room, knowing we needed to go to that evening's briefing so we knew what to expect for the next morning's disembarkation. We slowly stumbled up the stairs, feeling as weak as newborn baby deer and were greeted by cheerful friends! Pippa even stopped by to ask how we were feeling and many other staff checked on us as well. After the meeting, the ship ceased to rock and we felt solid.

The walls were lined with a guide's (Bill) drawings. He's so creative!

Could we make it to dinner without an emergency bathroom trip? Carefully, we plodded down the hallway with hope in our hearts and hunger in our bellies. We ate greedily and grateful to be back with everyone!

That evening we tried a new dessert called the Baked Alaskan and were surprised by the ice cream flavors, strawberry, coffee, and banana?? Everything was a blissful, tummy relieving blessing and we were grateful to once again be among the living.

We packed our bags and Mike played cards until the wee hours, knowing it'd be our last with our friends for awhile — we hope to see even more continents with them!
