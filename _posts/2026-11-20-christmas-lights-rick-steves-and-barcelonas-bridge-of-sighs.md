Two Barcelona tours down and one to go! Rick Steves is back to walk us through the historic district of Barcelona 

On our way over, we saw this: 

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1795869100/itmq8uuefsdnvv3cmjhz.jpg)

It looks like a park in the middle of the tram tracks! Its actually a vegetated track bed! It reduces noise, lessens the heat (as opposed to cement), and helps with traffic because it signals to cars and pedestrians that this is a tram track only 🚋🚃

We began our last tour in Plaça de Catalunya, the main square of the city! This is Barcelona's "Time Square". They use that term kindof loosely here. Both Spain's "Times Squares" had crowds and department stores but no hustlers, tons of characters, massive group tours, food stalls randomly filling up sidewalks, so many people you couldn't walk anywhere fast, or stories-high digital billboards with advertisements flashing every 15 seconds. But I'm sure they'll get there!

Heading towards the Gothic District, we stopped at a monument that symbolizes this region of Spain: Catalunya. 

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1795867385/nn9zrnwblcelvebnujc4.jpg "There are two theories regarding the shape of the monument: 1) it is the shape of Catalunya on the map and 2) a reminder that Catalunya's future is unfinished, and must be continued, one step at a time.")

There are many who wish Catalunya to be independent from Spain. They have their own culture, language, and flag and still gather here to protest their union with Spain. 

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1795867722/bpnjxp6s1mqapp0akahk.jpg "The Catalan flag")

We enjoyed walking down the Ramblas but it will probably be even more beautiful when the construction is finished 🚧

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1795868078/wbysdtgagqhnxzgxoenz.jpg)

This architecture is stunning! Beautiful gothic stone work and lovely balconies. Check out the roofs, you'll see the pully system used to help people move in (especially heavy furniture) as these buildings don't have elevators!

Down a side alley, we found the Els Quatre Gats — The four cats and we learned that Picasso used to hang out here and nurse drinks! Check out the beautiful hanging lantern with a dragon on it 🐲

Back out on the main pedestrian-only walkway, we find the most beautiful public water fountain ⛲

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1795869311/xofpqj8du8g5kqcmhudy.jpg "Even in the 1940s, 10% of Barcelonians had to get their water from public fountains due to a lack of indoor plumbing. ")

Peaking through the narrow walkways, we see the highlight of the tour: Barcelona Cathedral.
