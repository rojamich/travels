In planning this trip to Georgia, we knew much of it would be living our new, day-to-day lives and figuring out what that means in this country. We had a handful of days planned (museums, landmarks, cathedrals, and a few must-sees around the city. 

Instead of picking hard dates and booking them in advance (as we usually do), we decided to choose when felt best for us depending on:

* soreness
* energy
* weather
* excitement

Two days ago we saw there would be a break in the weather and very overcast. That, combined with it being a weekday, would make for a super day to tour Gori: Stalin's birthplace. 

Unfortunately, we stayed up way too late the night before and that meant for a brutal early alarm this morning. We dragged ourselves out of bed, ate a delicious breakfast, and decided to walk to the meet up point (stopping along the way for snacks, of course!). The meeting point was next to the metro station (how is public transportation so amazing here??) and the khinkali house ... will we successfully cook dinner tonight or succumb to the delicious temptations of Georgian food... stay tuned to find out!

There were people already gathered by a fountain in the square as we approached and we noticed vans and a huge tour bus in the parking lot - how big is this tour group??

Thankfully, it's a common meet up point for tours here and we were directed to a tiny van with only 7 travelers. Being early, we got good seats in the van with a table! We had loaded up audiobooks and sat back to enjoy the ride. Our tour guide, Oko was so passionate (maybe a little new/nervous) and excited for the trip! He shared a lot of information with us like:

* The name for the country of Georgia is actual Sakartvelo. Per the usual, we do not address them by their actual name (see Deutschland for Germany, Nihon for Japan, Misr for Egypt, and the list is really endless). 
* *These are called exonyms - the names given to it by foreigners. Endonyms are what the country calls itself.* 
* Georgia is globally considered the birthplace of wine! With 8,000 years of winemaking tradition and the word wine itself having derived from the Georgia word ghvino*!* 
* The name Georgia may come from an old Persian word, *gurğān*, which means the "land of wolves" because King Vakhtang I of Iberia (an old Georgian kingdom) was called Gorgasali, meaning "wolf-head," because his battle helmet had a wolf design - how interesting is that??

I won't overload you with information now, I'll sprinkle it in between the sights! But I did try a new chip flavor - its not as new as others but still different and good!

UNESCO refuses to protect the site as but is "officially reviewing" despite the request being submitted in 2007. There have been delays due to politics and preservation challenges.

Mike went inside the church to explore it - while I did pack a long sleeve, skirt, and there were head scarves available.. my skirt and long-sleeve were in the car. I could use theirs but Mike said he would check it out first to see if it was worth it. It wasn't really. 

Our guide explained to us that men take off head gear in the church to show they are equal to all and women cover their hair to cover their beauty... sounds more to me like women have to show they're not equal... don't get me started on religion and the patriarchy. 

We passed a well that was used at the time as a local jail. About 8 meters down, individuals were put down there as punishment, with their crimes listed above the well. The top was slitted to allow passersby to spit, defecate, and urinate on them. They were in the hole for roughly 4-5 days at a time.

But one stood out, not only because of it's size, but because of the woman it was dedicated to..."Queen Tamar's Hall" 👸🏻👑 aka Tamar the Great!

During her reign, she achieved so much for the country that, that time period is known as the "Georgian Golden Age"! She's also a saint in the Eastern Orthodox Church.

She expanded the kingdom, was considered a king, and outlawed the death penalty and torture!

She divorced her husband which was a major no no - not only for that time period but also in the church. She was able to persuade the church to formally break the bonds and he was exiled based on the accusations of: drunkenness, violent behavior, and homosexuality. He also plotted against Tamar rather than support her and that marriage had been arranged to try and control Tamar.

Did that turn out the way they thought it would? Nope.
