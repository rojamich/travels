Looking at our wanderlog — the site we use to keep track of our travel itineraries, we decided to use our last morning in this area to explore the Cistern Basilica because it looks amazing! Plus, it is highlighted in one of Mike's favorite books: [Inferno](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/17212231) by Dan Brown.

It was time to try another Turkish food, this time it was their "Turkish Bagel" aka Simit. It was really fresh, soft bread and could've used some cream cheese lol!

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1788372117/jonbm2x8rp6fycki4pmq.jpg)

We walked past this shop and thought, wait, wasn't this primarily in Morocco?

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1788372161/rykjxpubpu6c0en9waf0.jpg)

Turns out the fez (the tiny red hat), was assigned to the Ottoman military (1820s) as as symbol of equality and was later banned by Mustafa Kemal Atatürk under something called the Hat Law in 1925! The more you know 🌈

We followed the crowds to the entrance of the cistern (google maps takes you to the exit 🤦🏻‍♀️) and waited in line to buy tickets. Once inside, we were in awe at how ominous the golden hues with green undertones lit up the underground cathedral of water.

The different hues were all so diverse and the columns were too! There are 336 marble columns and they were repurposed from ancient pagan temples and Roman structures. How interesting to reuse them here.

Apparently, they turned her upside down/sideways to neutralize her haunting gaze 👻

Do you see the green-glowing column towards the back?

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1788373843/zleld1tgjddk6uacmaxl.jpg)

It is called the crying column to symbolize the thousands of slaves who suffered or died during the cistern construction. 

These contrasting colors really blew our minds!
