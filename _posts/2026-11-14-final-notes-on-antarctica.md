Before embarking on this life-changing adventure, Mike and I sat in our home office and asked ourselves, what would you regret missing out on in life? What places or experiences are bucket lists for us?

Gorilla trekking? The Galapagos? Diving with whale sharks? Son Doong Cave? Seeing all seven continents? The Pyramids? The Great Wall? Hot Air ballooning over Cappadocia?

We ranked them based on our excitement level, the cost, what physical agility was needed, and if we thought the site/experience was at risk of becoming impossible to complete/see. 

The ones we were most excited for:

1. Gorilla Trekking
2. Galapagos Islands
3. Antarctica

The ones we were most concerned would be at risk:

1. Antarctica 
2. Gorilla Trekking
3. The Great Wall

The greatest physical agility demand:

1. The Galapagos Islands
2. The Great Wall
3. Polar Bear Road Trip/Train Trip in Canada

The most costly:

1. Antarctica
2. The Pyramids
3. Swimming with Orca whales

Before watching [Allison Anderson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EDap9qxb96k) travel to Antarctica a few years ago, we didn't realize this was a feasible trip people actually went on other than expeditioners and scientists, of course! We dismissed it as out of the realm of possibility and something we could wait on 'til retirement. We'd have more money saved up and the physical demand wouldn't be bad.

But now, after looking at our list, we knew we'd need to prioritize it. Mike reached out to the company to get an estimate and began working with an agent to make this dream become a reality! We were really glad we did too, because 1) there will probably always be another place to use that money on and if we didn't commit, would we ever? 2) The physical demand was actually more than we expected. Part of that might be the expedition-style we chose, making it more affordable but also adventurous! But there are some things that you can't get away from. The biggest one we saw was the difficulty getting on and off the ship. It wasn't just an easy stroll (or wheelchair) down the gangway and off to snap a few photos. It was climbing in and out of the zodiacs, snowshoeing across the frozen tundra, hiking steep hills/mountains. And would we realistically want to cross Drake's Passage or jump in those frigid waters when we are retirement age? Would it even be possible for us?

This led us to Oceanwide Expeditions! Excited by the possibility to mountaineer, kayak, and possibly camp on the continent, we locked up a reservation for their first expedition of the 2026 season on the English speaking m/v Ortelius! We had to get insurance for the trip and even rent gear that would be delivered straight to the boat. This kept us from having to lug around parkas rated for freezing weather through summer in Armenia!

It was summer when we stepped foot on our seventh continent together: only four years after starting international travel together 🌏

And, we thought it would be colder! After freezing our butts off in Alaska in March, we were just fine for Antarctica's summer. We'd had to decide when to see Antarctica too, November-February is their typical cruising season and that influences what you'll see. We opted for the pristine white landscapes of early season over the later season of seeing baby penguins but everything and most penguins being poo-saturated. 

It was amazing to have it snow so often. Even though this changed our plans frequently, it made the experience more magical and it *felt* like what'd you expect of Antarctica. 

The food was excellent and plentiful when we weren't surviving off saltines. They had vegan, vegetarian, gluten free and meat-filled option at most meals and I never skimped because of options. I found a cheese I fell in love with (Boursin) and had nutella toast alongside every breakfast. 

The ship provided 1.5GB of free wifi a day and there were packages for purchase if you wanted more. We also had $100 USD credit each to spend on ship for drinks (alcohol or soft drinks), wifi, laundry, and souvenirs. The laundry was same say turn around and all we had to do was bag and itemize it — it was clean and ready at our cabin door when we returned from our afternoon activities!

We are SO happy we embarked on this journey. Was the drake hard? Yes, it was miserable, but was it worth it?

A million times yes. Absolutely!! 🥾🐧🛶🗻 

If we could change anything about the journey it would probably be to bring heaters for my toes (like hot packs) and another camera body for both of my lenses so I could go back and forth without having to choose a lens for the day. Of course, we'd change the seasickness if we could, but we did everything in our power to stay well. 

Overall it was an incredible adventure! We got to visit places we never considered like the southern-most postal office 📬Learned what sea ice sounds like when you kayak through it 🛶We saw *so* much wildlife — surpassing our wildest expectations with the whales 🐳and fearlessly dived into the icy waters of the Antarctic ocean 🌊 

Thank you immensely for joining us on this journey of a lifetime! Please join us for our next adventure: Our Spanish Home. We'll see you there 🙋🏻‍♂️🙋🏻‍♀️
