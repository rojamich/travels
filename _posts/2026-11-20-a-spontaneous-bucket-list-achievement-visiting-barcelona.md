Its an early morning here in Madrid for the Rojas family as we make our way to the train station for the 620am train! It was about a 30 minute walk and we hustled over. We can’t tell… are the streets watered down at night for cleanliness or did it rain?? My money is on raining but Mike says cleaning. It’s both rained here off and on, and we’ve seen people hosing down the street outside their business. But the main road is so heavily “watered” that it’s filled with puddles!

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1795724001/wk3nqiylyswgy9kyc3qp.jpg)

The station was relatively empty at this hour and only one train was present and being boarded. We sat down stairs after security and waited… and waited… and started getting suspicious. Our train was in the station, no one was around us, and it was 10 minutes til departure. We got up to investigate and asked a security guard. He jogged to ask a colleague and spoke Spanish, motioning to the far end of the hallway. We didn’t fully understand and walked that direction while searching. He shouted after us, “quince! Correo!” Meaning gate 15 and RUN! We hustled down the hallway (Mike with a boiling hot cup of coffee in one hand and a bag packed full of snacks in the other) and me with a backpack, towards the stairs. Because, of course it was stairs. We hit the second level and ran down the other side’s escalator to board the train. In our seats, the train started moving two minutes later. 

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1795723998/ddrf5kjbueemw5ximmus.jpg)

We still aren’t totally sure why one train boarded at our seating level but ours boarded from the second floor…just to walk back downstairs! I worked on the blog and sorted photos from our trip to Antarctica! I have been very behind on the blogging thanks to all the seasickness and data limitations! 

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1795724003/eb8uewd97x6b3luqgiqx.jpg)

It was a fun train ride! We loved it! If only it were longer, I could get more done! I thought the 3.5 hours would feel long after so much travel lately but, honestly, I really did need more time. There was a train delay so we actually got four hours lol.

Our train pulled into the station at 10am and we went right into another Rick Steve's tour — Spain is full of them! We only had so much time before our scheduled tour of Sagrada Familia. Stepping out into the street, it was much warmer here than in Madrid! About 19.444°*C* (67°f), compared to 12°C (55°f) in Madrid. The tour started in Eixample, Barcelona's fanciest neighborhood, on Passeig de Gracia, which is the main street.

And fancy it certainly was, we saw Montblanc (which I learned is not just a mountain or dessert, but also a high-end brand) and Jimmy Choo, which I tried not to look directly at, in case they charged me 😂.

The first stop was La Pedrera aka Casa Mila — an enormous mansion built by Antoni Gaudi.

{% gallery_block %7B%22photos%22%3A%5B%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795789698%2Ffm7e9lbndqs6jt5saz9u.jpg%22%7D%2C%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795789708%2Fzb8ifwr8sctonetf9gr8.jpg%22%7D%5D%2C%22caption%22%3A%22It%20even%20wraps%20around%20the%20corner!%22%7D %}

One of Gaudi's trademark designs is the bone-like columns 🦴🦴 Even though the design looks like melting ice cream of stone, it still has a steel skeleton. 

The neighborhood was remarkable 🤩 We were dazzled at every corner by the architecture and how each building was different.

And those balconies 😲😲

Barcelona was originally condensed into the Gothic Quarter and considered a slum before they tore down the walls and leveled this land to build the works of art you see now!

The streets had wide sidewalks, protected bike lanes, and hexagonal intersections to show off the buildings' designs 🖼️ They have a thriving gay community here and that neighborhood is called Gayxample — playing off of the Eixample neighborhood name 😂

The next stop was the Fundacio Antoni Tapies, it's an art museum, but looks like a prison who's barbed-wire installer had an accident.

Now, onto my *favorite* part of the tour! No, its not food, though, I'm surprised its not as well. It's the Casa Batllo and I can't believe I've never heard or seen  anything about it before!

{% gallery_block %7B%22photos%22%3A%5B%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795791196%2Fe6infghegcrscclwtbu5.jpg%22%7D%5D%2C%22caption%22%3A%22%22%7D %}

Check out that massive bay window at the bottom! And see how the columns are shaped like bones, classic Baudi, am I right?? The windows on the edges are kinda egg-shaped 🥚But I really love the balconies and how it has mosaic features 😍 Those shards are actually from discarded, shattered items like dishes, tiles, and China dolls 🤯

Stepping back, the roof is equally as mesmerizing!

{% gallery_block %7B%22photos%22%3A%5B%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795791196%2Fh5qunhxhu52kplhj3w65.jpg%22%7D%5D%2C%22caption%22%3A%22%22%7D %}

The tiles look iridescent, like the shiny rainbow-bubble quality you see 🫧and laid out like dragon scales.
