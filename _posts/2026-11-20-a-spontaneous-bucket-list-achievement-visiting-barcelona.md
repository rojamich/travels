Its an early morning here in Madrid for the Rojas family as we make our way to the train station for the 620am train! It was about a 30 minute walk and we hustled over. We can’t tell… are the streets watered down at night for cleanliness or did it rain?? My money is on raining but Mike says cleaning. It’s both rained here off and on, and we’ve seen people hosing down the street outside their business. But the main road is so heavily “watered” that it’s filled with puddles! Mike swears he saw a Zamboni specifically made for spraying the streets with water...

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1795724001/wk3nqiylyswgy9kyc3qp.jpg)

The station was relatively empty at this hour and only one train was present and being boarded. After going through security, we found Mike a coffee and snack and waited for our gate to be announced. It was finally allocated to gate 2 so we found seats in front of the gate to wait for boarding to begin. I got suspicious, noticing people milling around the train but Mike reassured me, saying it was probably just staff cleaning and preparing their first train of the day. We waited...and waited...no one was around us, and it was 10 minutes til departure. We got up to investigate and asked a security guard. He jogged to ask a colleague and spoke Spanish, motioning to the far end of the hallway. We didn’t fully understand and walked that direction while searching. He shouted after us, “quince! Correo!” Meaning gate 15 and RUN! We hustled down the hallway (Mike with a boiling hot cup of coffee in one hand and a bag packed full of snacks in the other) and me with a backpack, towards the stairs. Because, of course it was stairs. We hit the second level and ran down the other side’s escalator to board the train. In our seats, the train started moving two minutes later. 

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1795723998/ddrf5kjbueemw5ximmus.jpg)

We still aren’t totally sure why one train boarded at our seating level but ours boarded from the second floor…just to walk back downstairs! I worked on the blog and sorted photos from our trip to Antarctica! I have been very behind on the blogging thanks to all the seasickness and data limitations! 

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1795724003/eb8uewd97x6b3luqgiqx.jpg)

It was a fun train ride! We loved it! If only it were longer, I could get more done! I thought the 3.5 hours would feel long after so much travel lately but, honestly, I really did need more time. There was a train delay so we actually got four hours lol.

Our train pulled into the station at 1020am and we went right into another Rick Steve's tour — Spain is full of them! We only had so much time before our scheduled tour of Sagrada Familia. Stepping out into the street, it was much warmer here than in Madrid! About 19.4°*C* (67°f), compared to 12°C (55°f) in Madrid. The tour started in Eixample, Barcelona's fanciest neighborhood, on Passeig de Gracia, which is the main street. 

{% gallery_block %7B%22photos%22%3A%5B%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795789673%2Ffzcqjismaf1lvgmhjatw.jpg%22%7D%2C%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795789673%2Fudjhayyc75t8opqu5xpm.jpg%22%7D%5D%2C%22caption%22%3A%22%22%7D %}

And fancy it certainly was, we saw Montblanc (which I learned is not just a mountain or dessert, but also a high-end brand) and Jimmy Choo, which I tried not to look directly at, in case they charged me 😂.

The first stop was La Pedrera aka Casa Mila — an enormous mansion built by Antoni Gaudi.

{% gallery_block %7B%22photos%22%3A%5B%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795789698%2Ffm7e9lbndqs6jt5saz9u.jpg%22%7D%2C%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795789708%2Fzb8ifwr8sctonetf9gr8.jpg%22%7D%5D%2C%22caption%22%3A%22It%20even%20wraps%20around%20the%20corner!%22%7D %}

One of Gaudi's trademark designs is the bone-like columns 🦴🦴 Even though the design looks like melting ice cream of stone, it still has a steel skeleton. 

The neighborhood was remarkable 🤩 We were dazzled at every corner by the architecture and how each building was different.

{% gallery_block %7B%22photos%22%3A%5B%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795790186%2Fnqkpv6sa0tep7jv2cxvb.jpg%22%7D%2C%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795790187%2Fdwihmq1llgpsrsfdygak.jpg%22%7D%2C%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795790242%2Fimtgiqhsb7hmajyr4tgw.jpg%22%7D%2C%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795790265%2Fmfm3wmzjs8i8wklun8el.jpg%22%7D%5D%2C%22caption%22%3A%22%22%7D %}

And those balconies 😲😲

Barcelona was originally condensed into the Gothic Quarter and considered a slum before they tore down the walls and leveled this land to build the works of art you see now!

The streets had wide sidewalks, protected bike lanes, and hexagonal intersections to show off the buildings' designs 🖼️ They have a thriving gay community here and that neighborhood is called Gayxample — playing off of the Eixample neighborhood name 😂

The next stop was the Fundacio Antoni Tapies, it's an art museum, but looks like a prison who's barbed-wire installer had an accident.

Now, onto my *favorite* part of the tour! No, its not food, though, I'm surprised its not as well. It's the Casa Batllo and I can't believe I've never heard or seen  anything about it before!

{% gallery_block %7B%22photos%22%3A%5B%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795791196%2Fe6infghegcrscclwtbu5.jpg%22%7D%5D%2C%22caption%22%3A%22%22%7D %}

Check out that massive bay window at the bottom! And see how the columns are shaped like bones, classic Baudi, am I right?? The windows on the edges are kinda egg-shaped 🥚But I really love the balconies and how it has mosaic features 😍 Those shards are actually from discarded, shattered items like dishes, tiles, and China dolls 🤯

Stepping back, the roof is equally as mesmerizing!

{% gallery_block %7B%22photos%22%3A%5B%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795791196%2Fh5qunhxhu52kplhj3w65.jpg%22%7D%5D%2C%22caption%22%3A%22%22%7D %}

The tiles look iridescent, like the shiny rainbow-bubble quality you see 🫧and laid out like dragon scales.

Turning next door, you can see Casa Amatller:

{% gallery_block %7B%22photos%22%3A%5B%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795791195%2Fdjnkpcdvkqzrybmndyuf.jpg%22%7D%2C%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795791778%2Faqn5ivf3ez9szbnwv3ca.jpg%22%7D%5D%2C%22caption%22%3A%22%22%7D %}

This one was designed by Josep Puig i Cadafalch. Say that name five time fast.

The tiles are just an incredible pattern and look similar to Moorish palaces — we will get to see some of these in Morocco and I'm SO excited. The color combinations are really beautiful as well 🎨

The artist designed it with many A's hidden in the fascade to symbolize the Amatller family (who he designed it for). If you look at the lower right-hand side, you'll see the large, stone A over the balcony. Within the A are branches growing from an almond tree because Amatller means almond tree! Wow, what creativity! This family was also known as Barcelona's best chocolate makers. 

On the same block is Casa Lleo Morera and it has a lot of different architectural styles!

{% gallery_block %7B%22photos%22%3A%5B%7B%22x%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22y%22%3A%2250%22%2C%22image%22%3A%22https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdgw35sldo%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%2Cw_1200%2Fv1795793179%2Ff9qdlto2tzq5dmwenu8s.jpg%22%7D%5D%2C%22caption%22%3A%22%22%7D %}

I'm in love with this rounded bay window! Can you imagine this as a reading room? Bathed in sunlight and filled with cozy pillows?!

The building is spectacularly topped with this rooftop cupola! Yes, I had to look up what it was called 😂

Look how ornately decorated the roof's interior is!

Gosh, this street looks like something out of a painting! We can't believe we got to stroll down this 🏞️

We walked by a cultural center that "welcomes everyone" and has public restrooms! A lot of Europe charges for the bathroom here but we hadn't run into a restroom for cost here yet in Spain. I used the restroom and came out to find Mike had vanished... There was a puppet show going on down the hall so I went to search for him there. No sign of him. I waited there to text him when a woman speaking spanish hurried over to me. I understood zero of what she was saying but it felt like the puppet show required tickets so I went back down the halls to find Mike. I can now say I've been kicked out of a children's puppet show lol. Thanks honey 🐝😄 After looking around on the street, he emerged from the building after using the restroom on another level lol.

Back out on the street, we are directed to a high-end grocery store that has vintage ads for alcohol!

We searched for a snack but didn't find anything that struck our fancy. Why did they have to pick ham to love here? Why couldn't it be honey-drizzled walnuts?

Getting hungry, we were lucky to head to our last stop of the tour: the Church of the Holy Conception.

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1795795319/qpixgjjjjpg0otf3nflu.jpg)

Interestingly enough, you can't enter the front, you have to come around the side to a secret entrance. Are they ashamed we are wearing hoodies (looking properly trashy for Spain standards). On a serious note, everyone enters from the side. 

This is a 14th century church but sits in a neighborhood that didn't exist 150 years ago! This church was dismantled ***brick by brick*** and moved to this location. That is a *wild* amount of work but does have a beautiful courtyard at the entrance that looks ideal for meditation 🧘🏻‍♀️

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dgw35sldo/image/upload/c_limit,f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/v1795795318/gvds3nqnebocevrenpwu.jpg)

I also accidentally used the Starbucks men's restroom twice because the symbol looked too similar for men's / women's. Mike confirms the sign was unclear 😅
